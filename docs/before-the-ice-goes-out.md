# Before the Ice Goes Out

*A mystery where you are the one they're looking for.* Godot, built on Thespis 0.6.0 through its public SDK.
About 40 minutes a run, and no two runs alike.

## The story

Coldwater Landing, on the Yukon River, the spring of 1898. The gold rush filled the town and the ice shut it in:
from October to May nobody comes or goes. On the river sits a tripod tied by a line to a clock on the shore, and
every soul in town has a bet in the ice pool on the minute it moves.

Four nights ago Otto Brand's store burned: the trading post and assay office where he kept the grubstake notes.
Half the creek owed him a share of gold they hadn't found yet, and your sister Věra, widowed in the winter, owed
him her claim and her cabin. On the first of May he would have taken both. You are **Anton Holub**, and you set
the fire to burn the notes. You didn't know that Tamás, the night watchman, was sleeping off a bottle in the back.

He's alive. Burned, at the mission infirmary, and he doesn't remember the night. Not yet.

The last dog team over the ice brought **Inspector Gilchrist** of the North-West Mounted Police, the only law for a
thousand miles. He has no proof and doesn't look for any. He takes a room at the post, walks the town, and talks:
to the roadhouse keeper, to the wood boy, to the watchman's son, to you. He is polite, and he remembers every word.
When the ice goes out he leaves on the first steamer, and he takes someone with him: whoever he believes set that
fire.

The town is quietly glad the notes are ash. Everyone has something to hide about that night, and nobody is sorry
enough to help the Inspector. That is your cover, and it is also your danger.

## What the literature gives it

- **Dostoevsky, *Crime and Punishment*:** an investigator who never accuses and waits for the culprit to talk
  himself into a corner. Gilchrist is the game's one opponent, and you lose by what you say, not by what he finds.
- **Akutagawa, *In a Grove*:** every witness tells a version of the night that protects themselves. Nobody in
  Coldwater tells the Inspector the plain truth, and their lies can cover yours or expose it.
- **Shakespeare, *Othello*:** the best lie is a half-truth told to someone who wants to believe it, and the most
  chilling one is told to a man who can't check his own memory.
- **Gogol, *The Government Inspector*:** a town with something to hide believes what suits it. Coldwater would
  rather blame a stranger, and in a gold rush everyone is a stranger.
- **Poe, *The Tell-Tale Heart*:** guilt gives itself away. Deny what nobody has said, and he notices.
- **Jack London, *To Build a Fire*, and Robert Service's Yukon ballads:** the cold as a force that doesn't care,
  and fire as the thing everyone up here lives by and fears.

These works are public domain; we take their shapes and their weather, not their text or characters.

## The names

Every name is one a real person from that place could have carried in 1898, and its meaning in their own language
carries the story, the way Dostoevsky's *Raskolnikov* carries *raskol*, "schism". The game never explains them;
players who look them up find a second layer.

- **Anton Holub:** *holub* is Czech for "dove", innocence worn by an arsonist, and "pigeon", the easy mark. *Anton*
  recalls St Anthony, whose name is on the burning sickness called St Anthony's fire.
- **Věra Holub:** *věra* is "faith". Her arc is whether she keeps faith with her brother.
- **Inspector Ewan Gilchrist:** from Gaelic *Gille Chrìosd*, "servant of Christ". Less a hunter than a confessor,
  waiting for you to tell him.
- **Tamás Kovács:** Thomas, who wouldn't believe until he saw; *kovács* is "smith", a man of fire burned by it.
- **Sándor Kovács:** Alexander, "defender of men". He defends his father, and in the wrong hands, against the
  wrong man.
- **Otto Brand:** *ōt*, "wealth", and *Brand*, German for "fire". A fortune that burned.
- **Maggie Doyle:** the magpie is named for *Mag*, Margaret; it chatters and hoards bright things.
- **Billy Tuck:** what's tucked away is hidden, as what he saw is.
- **Martha Matthias:** the name she was given at baptism at the Forty Mile mission, as the Hän of 1898 were: the
  missions often made a father's Christian name the family's surname. Martha in the gospel is the one who works
  while the others talk, and the one who goes out and says the plain truth; the name means "the lady", in a town
  that treats her as a servant. Matthias was the apostle chosen by lot to take the betrayer's place, in a town run
  by a lottery that wants someone to take the culprit's. Her own name isn't in any record the town keeps, and the
  game doesn't invent one.
- **Jack Ashby:** an alias, an ordinary English surname with *ash* in it: the stranger the town would like to bury in
  the ashes. His real name is in the Inspector's Notebook.

## The people downriver

The Hän lived on this river long before the rush. They were researched for this game in
[before-the-ice-han-1898.md](before-the-ice-han-1898.md), from the Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in's own published history where
possible. Coldwater is fictional, but it follows what happened at Dawson:

- **Their fish camp was at the creek mouth**, where Věra's cabin and the claims are now. When the rush came they
  sold the miners their houses, believing the land wasn't part of the sale; the miners believed it was. The salmon
  traps were torn out. Over the winter the people agreed with the mission and the government to move three miles
  downriver, and this spring they are building cabins there. Their camp is off the model: Martha comes to town, and
  her home isn't a set to tour.
- **April isn't salmon season.** It's the end of the late-winter hunt, grayling and the first ducks, mending canoes
  and nets, tanning, and sewing. Moccasins have gone from 75 cents to seven dollars a pair this winter.
- **Martha's household fed the town.** Her brother's party brought in moose and caribou when the town was hungry,
  and Brand bought the meat on account, written in his ledger. The ledger burned with the notes, and Brand now says
  the account went with it. The fire that freed half the creek from its debts wiped out the one debt owed to her.
  (The account is the game's invention; the meat sales and the prices are on record.)
- **She was in Maggie's kitchen that night**, where she sleeps when it's too late to walk the river trail home and
  Maggie pays for gloves in board. At midnight she saw Maggie sell Tamás the bottle. At one she saw a man with a
  lantern go east along the lane past the window, and come back ten minutes later; on some seeds, his face.

So she can save you or hang you. What she saw of the bottle is the safest story in town, and it's true. What she saw
in the lane is the one sighting that could break your alibi without Věra. But her word travels badly: she has few
ties in town, so what she says stops at their edge unless someone she trusts carries it: Věra, who buys from her. Telling the police about the bottle brings them down on Maggie, who feeds her, and the police are the
ones who staked her people's land. Gilchrist, unlike the town, will take her statement and weigh it, if he hears
there's one to take. That makes her a choice for the player: you can make sure nobody hears her, and know what you
did.

She is never the one Gilchrist takes. The town may say it was "one of the Indians", and the Notebook names that for
the prejudice it is; no record ties any Dawson fire to the Hän, and the game doesn't add one.

**What the game doesn't use:** Hän words (the language is critically endangered and its teaching materials aren't
licensed for this), songs, the dancing stick, potlatch or other ceremony, the Moosehide Gathering (which began in
1993), or any real person. Chief Isaac stays offstage. Martha speaks plain, fluent English with her own opinions,
never the newspapers' pidgin. A Hän reader, paid and credited, reads her and sees her portrait before the game is released.

## How a day plays

Each of the five days has a morning, an afternoon and an evening. You choose where to go: the landing and its
tripod, Maggie's roadhouse, the burned store, the mission infirmary, the Mounted Police post, Věra's cabin up the
creek. You talk to whoever is there, in your own words or by choosing what to do: tell, ask, pay, comfort,
threaten, confess. Meanwhile Gilchrist makes his own rounds, and the town talks among itself.

**Every evening Gilchrist asks you to sit with him.** These conversations are the set pieces. His questions come from
what he has heard that day: *"Billy Tuck says he saw a lantern by Brand's back door. Yours, perhaps?"* You answer,
and he weighs it against what you told him before and what others have told him.

**The evenings work without a model.** Each of his questions is a written line, chosen by what he believes that day
and filled in with who told him and what they saw, so the line above comes from Billy's sighting, not from a
writer guessing it. Your answers go through `understand` as anywhere else: typed words are read against the game's
own phrases, and the suggested acts are always there. A model, when there is one, gives him his own voice on top of
the same question, but it never decides what he asks or what he concludes; those come from the engine either way,
so the case runs by the same rules with or without one.

## The town

Coldwater Landing sits on the south bank of the Yukon where Coldwater Creek comes in from the north. The claims are
up the creek, across the ice; all winter the ice is the road between them, and when it goes out the creek is cut
off until the boats run. Like Dawson's, the town is one street along the bank, Front Street, with the buildings
facing the river across it. Behind them runs a back lane of back doors and woodpiles with no lamps, and that is
where the night of the fire happened.

The plan is drawn on the design canvas ("The town plan"), with the references it's built from: Dawson's Front
Street, the Mounted Police compound at Fort Herchmer, St Mary's mission hospital, the Dawson ice pool (recorded since

### The places

You go to one place each morning, afternoon and evening. Walking between places in town is part of that turn;
getting across the river can cost more (below).

| # | Place | Who you'll find | What's there for the story |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The landing** | Billy (mornings), Ashby (all day), the pool crowd | The tripod and its clock, the ice-pool board, the laid-up steamer, Ashby's tent. Everyone at the landing sees who comes and goes over the ice |
| 2 | **Maggie's roadhouse** | Maggie, Otto, Sándor and Ashby (evenings); Martha in the kitchen (evenings) | The bar, the kitchen where Billy eats, the back room where Otto was fixing a card game the night of the fire |
| 3 | **Brand's store** | Otto, picking through the ashes (afternoons) | The ruin, the back door Billy saw the lantern by, the cordwood stack he was stealing from |
| 4 | **The mission infirmary** | Tamás, always; Sándor (mornings) | The church and its hospital at the east end, the window looking back along the street |
| 5 | **The Mounted Police post** | Gilchrist, when he isn't on his rounds | A small stockade compound with a flag, and his room. Every evening he asks you to sit with him here |
| 6 | **The ice** | Ashby, the curious | The tripod up close. Where the ice talks first as the week warms |
| 7 | **Věra's cabin** | Věra | At the creek mouth across the river, facing the town; the claim Brand would have taken is up the creek behind it. From her door she sees the landing and the lights of Front Street |
| 8 | **Your cabin** | You; Gilchrist's men, if it comes to that | West end of the street. Where each day starts, and what he searches when he starts to believe |

The Kovács forge, hard against Brand's east wall, is on the model but isn't a place you visit: Tamás is a smith by
trade (*kovács*) who watched the store by night, and Sándor sleeps there while his father is in the infirmary. A
forge next door is the accident the town would rather believe. The steamer isn't a place either, until the last day.

The layout comes from the story as much as from the history. Brand's ruin is the middle of town, opposite the
landing, so you pass what you did every day, and whoever lingers there is noticed (*Crime and Punishment*). To reach
Tamás you pass the Inspector's window, so every visit to the man who can't trust his own memory is seen
(*Othello*). Ashby lives in a tent in the open at the landing, where a town with something to hide finds its
stranger (*The Government Inspector*). And the back lane hides you until you keep to it too often (*The Tell-Tale
Heart*).

### The night of the fire

Drawn on the canvas as the same plan at one in the morning: where everyone really was, and the one piece each could
see. Nobody saw it all; the Inspector's Notebook is him piecing it together (*In a Grove*).

- **About 9 pm:** you cross the ice to Věra's. **11.30 pm:** you walk home across it with your lantern; she watches
  it all the way to the landing.
- **Midnight:** Maggie sells Tamás a bottle at her kitchen door; he takes it to his bed in Brand's back room.
  Martha, sewing by the stove, sees it.
- **12.30 am:** Otto is dealing a fixed game in the roadhouse back room.
- **1.00 am:** you go out your back door and along the lane, past Maggie's kitchen window, to Brand's back door,
  where the notes are kept. **1.10 am:** you come back the same way, past the back-room window.
- **1.15 am:** Ashby, stowing food aboard the laid-up steamer for his escape, sees the glow first and shouts.
- **1.20 am:** Sándor breaks in from the forge and drags his father out.

| Who | Saw | Why they can't say so |
|---|---|---|
| Věra | your lantern crossing home at 11.30, and the glow later | she keeps quiet while she trusts you |
| Billy Tuck | a lantern at Brand's back door, no face | he was stealing Brand's cordwood; to the police he was asleep |
| Otto Brand | a lantern going west past the back-room window | he was fixing a card game; to the police he was in his bed |
| Maggie Doyle | Tamás drunk at midnight, with her bottle | she sold it to him |
| Jack Ashby | the glow, first; he raised the alarm | he was stowing away on the steamer |
| Sándor Kovács | his father, burned | nothing; he wants someone to pay |
| Tamás Kovács | a lantern, a coat, a face in the firelight | he can't remember yet |
| Martha Matthias | Tamás buying the bottle; a man with a lantern in the lane at one, and back at ten past | nobody asks her, and the town won't carry her word |

Your story is that you were **at Věra's till late**. It's the natural lie: you were there, and across the ice is
the one place nobody in town can see. It only holds if your sister lies for you, because she watched you leave at
11.30, an hour and a half before the fire. Whether she will is her arc: *věra* is "faith", and she keeps quiet
while she trusts you. Tell it to Maggie and it's all over town by morning, including, sooner or later, to Věra
herself, and to Gilchrist, who will take his questions across the ice. Two others can break it. Otto saw
a lantern pass his back-room window going west, and he'd have to admit to the card game first. Martha saw it pass
Maggie's kitchen window, and the town would have to listen to her first.

### The ways between them

| Way | Joins | Seen by | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Front Street** | every place in town, and the landing across it | whoever is at the landing, on the roadhouse porch, at the post window or the infirmary window | none |
| **The back lane** | the back doors, from your cabin to the mission | only whoever is out back: Billy at the cordwood, the card players in the roadhouse back room, Martha at the kitchen window | none |
| **The ice crossing** | the landing and the creek | everyone at the landing and on the ice | none |
| **The bend crossing** | the west end and the creek, behind the spruce point | nobody | the next turn as well |

Being seen is the point. Whoever has a way in view when you pass now knows where you went, first-hand, and can say
so; the walk shows them as it happens. Front Street is in view of the whole town. The back lane is in view of almost
nobody, which is why it's where you went that night, and why the two who were out back (Billy at the cordwood, Otto
at his crooked card game) each saw something they can't admit to having been there to see. The bend crossing is the
only unseen way to Věra, and it costs you a turn.

The ice clock closes ways too. When the crack opens at the bend, the bend crossing is gone, and the only way to Věra
is past everyone at the landing. On the last day the ice moves, and the creek is cut off.

## Two clocks

**The ice.** The town's talk turns to the pool as the days warm: the river groans at night, a crack opens at the
bend. When the tripod moves, the steamer comes and Gilchrist boards it with whatever he believes.

**Tamás.** Each day he remembers a little more: the smell of coal oil, a lantern, a coat, then a face. You can sit
with him, and you can *help* him remember: tell him it was a stranger's lantern he saw. Early on he'll believe you,
because you're the one who visits. As his own memory hardens, what he saw counts for more than what he's told, and
if your story loses, he knows you lied to him. He can be the friend who forgives you or the witness who hangs you.
He trusts the one who sits with him: a day you don't come costs a little of that, and a day you do wins it back.
Let it run low and what he remembers of you goes to Sándor, and on the last day perhaps to the Inspector. On a bad
week, if nobody fetches the medicine, the fever takes him first, and you'll have to live with feeling
relieved.

**The pressure.** A third clock you don't see: each morning the game measures how close the town has come to you
(how far Gilchrist suspects you, whether your account has broken, how many people hold something true and damaging
about you) and sets it against a target that rises through the week. Fall behind it and something the night left
waiting comes out: a miner catches Otto dealing crooked cards and, cornered, Otto admits the lantern he saw; Brand
catches Billy at his cordwood again and the boy blurts what he saw, unless you've paid him; on the weeks Martha saw
your face, she's overheard saying so; the fever breaks early and Tamás remembers. Run far ahead of it on the first
days and the town looks elsewhere for a while: food stowed aboard the laid-up steamer turns eyes to Ashby; an empty
bottle in the ruin turns them to the watchman. The fourth morning is never quiet. And Gilchrist won't search a
cabin before the third day: the case has to grow before it bites. Every incident only brings out what someone truly
saw, or something true that happens that day. The numbers are in [`case.json`](../games/before-the-ice/case.json)'s
`director`, to be tuned in playtests.

## What makes it fun

- **You can feel his suspicion without a meter.** As he comes to believe it was you, his choices change: he takes
  his questions to Věra, has your cabin searched, tells you not to leave town. Each step is a warning you can read
  and answer.
- **Lying low isn't safe.** The case grows without you. The first evening he asks where you were at one; say
  nothing and that counts as no account, which with a lantern in the lane is enough to warn you. He crosses the ice
  to ask your sister either way, and what she says depends on how far she trusts you. Leave Tamás alone and what he
  remembers finds its own way out. Hiding is a choice with a price, like any other.
- **Lies have a life of their own.** A story told to Maggie is all over town by morning. Told to Sándor, it becomes a
  fight at the landing. It can also come back to you by a route you didn't foresee.
- **Getting caught is a turn of the plot, not the end.** If a truer story beats yours, whoever heard your lie trusts
  you less. That includes Věra, and with her trust goes her silence.
- **Witnesses are people, not clues.** Billy can be paid, befriended or frightened; each has a cost. Paying him
  too much makes him wonder why.
- **Everyone else has something to hide.** Otto says he was in his bed; he was dealing in Maggie's back room, and
  Maggie and Martha know it. Billy says he was asleep; he was at Brand's back door with the cordwood, and someone
  may have seen him. Point a story at either and Gilchrist goes to ask where he was at one. Once he hears the truth
  after the lie, any story pointing that way will do to take him.
- **The true thing is the best weapon.** Tamás *was* drunk. Maggie *did* sell him the bottle. "An old man, a bottle
  and a lamp" is the safest story in town, and it's mostly true.
- **Saying too much gives you away.** Deny the fire before anyone has asked and Gilchrist writes it down.

## How it ends

There's no scripted finale. When the ice goes out, Gilchrist boards the steamer with whatever he believes:

- **Breakup.** He's satisfied it was the watchman's lamp. The town cheers the ice, and nobody speaks of it again.
- **Another Man's Coat.** He takes Jack Ashby, or Otto, or Billy. You're free, and the epilogue shows what it cost.
- **A Hand on the Shoulder.** He takes you, at the landing, in front of the whole town.
- **The Confession.** You tell him the truth and why. He believes the why, and the sentence is a mercy.
- **Věra.** If she's sure you're lost, and loves you enough, she confesses to save you.
- **Tamás Remembers.** He forgives you, or he goes to Gilchrist, and either way the decision was his.

Then comes **the Inspector's Notebook**: everything he came to believe, who told him each thing and when, the road
your lie took through town, and the one sentence that turned him. This is the screen people will share, and the
reason they play again.

## Why they play again

The seed changes who saw what, who is tied to whom, how fast Tamás remembers, and which of the town's secrets is
true. The lie that worked last time is the one that hangs you this time. Each seed draws a week from
[`case.json`](../games/before-the-ice/case.json)'s `week`:

- **Who saw the lantern** besides Martha: Billy and Otto, or only one of them (Otto never looked up from his cards;
  Billy ran with the wood).
- **Whether Martha saw the face**, about one week in three.
- **When Tamás sees a face again**, day 3, 4 or 5, or never; and **whose**. Usually Anton's. Sometimes, in the fever,
  it's Ashby's, the man who shouted fire and pulled at the door beside him. He is sure, and he's wrong. Sitting with
  him, Anton hears it; on the last day Tamás sends for the Inspector.
- **What the town said the morning after**: nothing yet, or a story someone had first. At Maggie's it was the
  stranger; Brand blames his watchman's lamp; downriver they say Brand burned his own book, to be rid of what he
  owed; Maggie heard the wood boy was at the back door. Anton hears it that first morning. It is a story, not a
  sighting, and it travels like any other.
- **What Věra saw.** Usually that he left at half past eleven. Sometimes, up with the glow, she saw his lantern come
  back over the ice. She keeps it while she trusts him; if he loses her, it goes into town. If he's lost and she
  loves him enough, she takes it on herself.
- **Whom Martha talks to**: Věra, and on some weeks Maggie too, across the kitchen, so what she saw goes round.
- **Who saw Billy at the back door**: Otto from the back-room window (his wood, again), Martha from the kitchen, or
  nobody.
- **Whether Maggie keeps her back room quiet.** Usually she says who was there. On some weeks she takes a cut of
  Otto's game, and keeps it to herself.

The seed draws again until the week keeps the rules. Every week gives the town someone else to suspect: a story,
or Tamás's wrong face on a day that comes. Gilchrist never knows anything nobody told him. A run takes about 40
minutes, every ending can be reached, and none comes from a trigger: it comes from what nine people came to believe.

Whether that holds for players is the playtest's question: [the playtest kit](before-the-ice-playtest.md) has the
seeds, the session and the questions, and every week writes a record to set beside the player's own account.

## How it looks

A town on a desk. Everything is an object under one coal-oil lamp at night: Coldwater is a miniature on a walnut
desk, its plinth cut away to show the river ice; the interface is paper on the desk (a cartouche, the ice-pool
ticket, pinned flags, ledger slips, a ring-bound casebook). Red only ever means a connection: string from teller to
listener, your route, the road a rumour took. Screens are drafted on the design canvas: the town, the walk, a
conversation, the casebook, the ending.

- **Pixel style, built in 3D.** The town is modelled in Blender (the scripts are in
  [`art/before-the-ice`](../art/before-the-ice/README.md)) and drawn at low resolution with banded light (warm lamplight, cold blue shadow) and 1-pixel
  outlines, then scaled up whole-pixel. In Godot this runs live: render the 3D town to a low-resolution viewport
  and scale it up, so the camera, the walk and the day's light still move. The paper stays at full resolution on
  top.
- **People are pixel figures** (32x48) standing in the model in their own colours; portraits in conversation are
  larger half-length pixel cut-outs with a paper-white edge. The figures and portraits are where an artist matters
  most; the drafts are stand-ins.
- **Movement is by choosing, not steering.** Pick a flag on the model and Anton walks the route in a few seconds,
  skippable. The walk counts: whoever sees you pass now knows where you went (a first-hand sighting), and a longer
  back way goes unseen.
- **The casebook turns like paper.** In Godot the page is a deformable mesh that curls from the corner you drag
  (release past halfway to finish the turn, before it to let it fall back), lit by the lamp, casting a soft shadow
  on the page beneath, with a paper sound. The canvas draft only rotates the page flat.

## To decide before building

- Martha's name. Her working name was Martha Paul, but *Paul* is the surname of a living Hän family at Eagle, one
  of the river's Hän communities, so she is Martha Matthias. A web search (November 2026) found no Hän or Yukon
  family of that name; the registers that would settle it (the Anglican Diocese of Yukon's, the 1901 census for
  Moosehide) aren't online, so the Hän reader, or the Tr'ondëk Hwëch'in Heritage department, checks it.
